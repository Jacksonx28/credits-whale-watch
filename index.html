This dashboard watches how Credits move and how collectors turn them into Statements. A smaller supply is measurable. Future demand is not.

An independent project by Jackson ↗
Credits is an artwork by Jack Butcher.

What counts as a whale movement?

A Credit transfer between two addresses involving a monitored holder with at least 80 Credits at the snapshot block, or a transfer of at least 80 Credits in one transaction. Transfers are grouped by transaction, sender and receiver. Burns, minting and self-transfers are excluded. This does not identify a person or prove buying, selling or intent.

How are burns and Statements counted?

Statement history is replayed from the deployed contract’s Ethereum Transfer logs. Every original Statement mint is matched to 80 Credit burns in the same transaction. Composed is the cumulative mint count; circulating excludes burned Statements. Overprints use the contract’s Overprinted event. All-time history starts at Statement deployment, block 26,100,733.

How fresh and complete is this data?

The collector is scheduled hourly through GitHub Actions. The page checks for a newer snapshot every minute. Runs can be delayed; a snapshot older than 90 minutes is marked stale. Reads use a block 12 blocks behind the chain head, which reduces reorganisation risk but is not absolute finality. The feed covers the latest 48 hours. Holder candidates come from Blockscout’s top 100; balances are then verified at the snapshot block. Indexer lag may omit a newly large holder, so this is a monitored cohort, not a complete holder census. If collection fails, the last successful snapshot stays visible with its age.

Where does Balance fit?

Jack’s proposed Balance design would lock a Statement and release its rating as divisible tokens, while wallet artwork reflects the balance and mixed colors. Divisibility does not guarantee buyers or liquidity. Balance is not tracked here until a deployed contract and its mechanics can be verified.
