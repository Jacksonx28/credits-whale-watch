Jack’s Balance proposal / explained simply

The tokens move.
The artwork keeps its memory.

Based on the note you shared from Jack. This explains a proposed design; it is not a launch announcement.

Proposed design
01

Credits are the individual marks.

A private $8 X Money payment became a Credit: its transaction ID was hashed into CMYK printing plates. A fixed rating measures traits, not what someone will pay.

02

80 marks become one page.

A collector burns 80 Credits to compose a Statement. Their ratings and colors combine. Overprinting combines two Statements and burns one of them.

03

The page could become something divisible.

Under Balance, a Statement would be permanently locked. Every rating point would release one transferable ERC-20 Balance token. The Statement’s art and history would remain, but it could no longer move.

04

The money-like part travels. The art stays.

Each receiving wallet would get a non-transferable artwork showing its current Balance. Send the tokens and the art updates. At zero, the art still exists. Colors mix by amount and carry aggregate ancestry; they do not identify every original Credit. Exchanges can mix those colors further.

Jack’s central idea: the market becomes part of the artwork. Trading redistributes its quantity and colors; if trading stops or its price disappears, the artwork and its history still exist.

Why this matters for the whale watch: composing removes Credits, overprinting removes Statements, and a future surrender would remove Statements from circulation while releasing Balance. Each is a different choice. Holding 80 Credits creates an option; an actual burn shows that option being used.

More divisibility does not create buyers. Jack is describing how the artwork could move through a market, not promising a price or liquidity.

What makes it into the Statement watch?

The collector cohort uses indexed addresses without contract code, holding 80+ Credits now or before a qualifying change. Additions and reductions are netted per wallet and transaction. A change qualifies when it moves at least eight Credits (one tenth of a Statement), or crosses a whole 80-Credit bundle boundary—even if just one Credit completes the bundle. Verified Credit burns matched to Statement mints also qualify. Generic Statement transfers, overprints, self-transfers, small capacity-neutral changes, and indexed contract activity are excluded from this feed. Contracts may include legitimate smart wallets, so this scope does not cover every collector. Transfers do not establish a purchase, sale, or intention to burn.

How are burns and Statements counted?

Statement history is replayed from the deployed contract’s Ethereum Transfer logs. Every original Statement mint is matched to 80 Credit burns in the same transaction. Composed is the cumulative mint count; circulating excludes burned Statements. Overprints use the contract’s Overprinted event. All-time history starts at Statement deployment, block 26,100,733.

How fresh and complete is this data?

The collector is scheduled hourly through GitHub Actions. The page checks for a newer snapshot every minute. Runs can be delayed; a snapshot older than 90 minutes is marked stale. Reads use a block 12 blocks behind the chain head, which reduces reorganisation risk but is not absolute finality. The feed covers the latest 48 hours. Holder candidates come from Blockscout’s top 100; balances are then verified at the snapshot block. Holding signals replay all Credit transfers, mints and burns in the 48-hour window. “Held 80+ for 48h” means the verified balance never fell below 80 during that window. Position change includes burns and small transfers, even when those transfers are omitted from the feed. Indexer lag may omit a newly large holder, so this is a monitored cohort, not a complete holder census. If collection fails, the last successful snapshot stays visible with its age.

Where does Balance fit?

Jack’s proposed Balance design would lock a Statement and release its rating as divisible tokens, while wallet artwork reflects the balance and mixed colors. Divisibility does not guarantee buyers or liquidity. Balance is not tracked here until a deployed contract and its mechanics can be verified.
