02 The thesis

Scarcity is a choice,
before it becomes a number.

Supply can shrink. That does not automatically make the remaining art more valuable. Both sides start with the same question: is a Statement worth the sacrifice?

Two sides of the same burn.
+

Fewer Credits. More reasons to finish.

Someone holding 72 Credits needs just eight more to make a Statement. If enough holders complete their bundles while larger collectors burn, available Credits could tighten.

The reported 80+ cohort can already support 350 Statements: 28,000 Credits removed, or almost 23% of the original supply. Participation could create a meaningful change without thousands of wallets needing to coordinate.

A second layer of scarcity

Statements can also be overprinted. The top Statement is burned in that process, so supply can fall at both levels. Holders may also preserve rarer Credits and assemble with common ones; that is a possibility, not observed burn behavior.

−

The burn needs a reason to happen.

Assembly is voluntary. If a Statement is worth less to a holder than the 80 Credits it costs to create, that holder can simply keep or sell their Credits.

Large holders can create many Statements. Their participation reduces Credit supply while adding new Statement inventory. Scarcity only supports the thesis when there is demand for the result.

What could weaken the thesis?

The launch may already be reflected in Credit prices. Limited burning, weak Statement demand or heavy selling by large holders would weaken the expected supply squeeze. Fewer tokens alone do not guarantee a higher price.

Sources & how to read the numbers

The 80-Credit assembly mechanic, launch time and 1,526 maximum are from jack.art. The playground is an independent visual experiment using generated patterns, not collection artwork or onchain tokens.

The holder baseline is carried forward from the original research, which attributed its distribution to a public holder analysis. Its exact snapshot time and wallet-level dataset have not been verified in this redesign. The 613 figure covers 40–79, not 50–79; 224 includes all wallets at 80 or more.

350 is the earlier reported sum of whole bundles per wallet. It cannot be confirmed by dividing the combined 32,783 holdings by 80, because credits left over in one wallet cannot complete another wallet's bundle. The supply explorer uses the original 122,153 baseline and illustrates scenarios only.
